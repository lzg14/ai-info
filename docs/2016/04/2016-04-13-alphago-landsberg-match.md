In April 2016, Google DeepMind's AlphaGo continued its dominance in the Go world by defeating Ke Jie, the world's top-ranked Go player at the time, in a unofficial match played on the online gaming platform Tenego. Ke Jie, a 19-year-old Chinese prodigy who had held the world number one ranking for two years, expressed confidence before the match but was ultimately defeated by AlphaGo's unconventional strategies. The online encounter came just weeks after AlphaGo's historic victory over Lee Sedol in Seoul, demonstrating that the AI system continued to improve and adapt its playing style. Ke Jie reportedly spent considerable time preparing for the match, studying AlphaGo's previous games for weaknesses, but found the machine's play style impossible to predict or counter. The match consisted of two games, with AlphaGo winning both, though Ke Jie managed to push the AI to calculate harder in certain positions than it had against Lee Sedol. This victory solidified AlphaGo's reputation as the strongest Go player in the world, human or machine, and raised important questions about the future of competitive Go. DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis announced that the company was considering additional official matches against top professionals, eventually leading to the future AlphaGo vs Ke Jie matches that would take place in 2017. The news generated enormous interest in China, where Go has a passionate following, accelerating the adoption of AI technologies across Chinese tech companies and research institutions.
