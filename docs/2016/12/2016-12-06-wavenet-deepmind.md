DeepMind unveiled WaveNet in December 2016, a deep generative model that produced remarkably human-like speech and music audio. The system represented a fundamental shift in how computers approach audio generation, moving away from traditional concatenative text-to-speech methods that relied on recorded snippets of human speech. WaveNet used a convolutional neural network architecture that processed raw audio waveforms pixel by pixel, learning to generate each sample based on all previous samples. This approach allowed the model to capture the nuanced rhythms, intonations, and textures that make human speech sound natural. In blind tests, human evaluators rated WaveNet-generated speech as significantly more natural than existing state-of-the-art text-to-speech systems, with some participants even struggling to distinguish it from real human recordings. Beyond speech, WaveNet demonstrated the ability to generate music, producing piano compositions that displayed remarkable coherence and musicality. The model's architecture, called PixelCNN, treated audio generation similarly to image generation by using dilated causal convolutions that could process long sequences with receptive fields spanning tens of thousands of timesteps. Despite its impressive results, the original WaveNet required massive computational resources, taking about 90 minutes to generate just one second of audio. However, the research paved the way for more efficient architectures and found immediate applications in Google products, eventually powering the Google Assistant and Google Translate's audio features. WaveNet exemplified how deep learning could transform creative and perceptual tasks that were previously thought to require human intuition.
