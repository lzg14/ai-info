OpenAI made headlines in August 2017 by demonstrating that its reinforcement learning agents could achieve competitive performance in complex video games, most notably the popular strategy game Dota 2. The OpenAI Five project, which would later become famous for defeating professional human players, began showing promising results in 2017, showcasing the potential for reinforcement learning to master games of imperfect information with long time horizons and complex team dynamics.

The approach used by OpenAI involved training multiple agents to play as a team, learning to cooperate and compete through self-play, a technique where agents improve by playing against copies of themselves. This method had previously proven effective in games like Atari and Go, but Dota 2 presented additional challenges due to its scale, complexity, and the need for coordination among team members.

OpenAI's Dota 2 bots learned to play the game from scratch, developing strategies and techniques that were in some cases different from those employed by human players. The agents demonstrated the ability to coordinate their actions, make long-term plans, and adapt to opponent strategies in real-time. These capabilities required advances in both the underlying algorithms and the computational infrastructure used to train the agents.

The significance of these results extended beyond gaming applications. The techniques being developed for game-playing AI had direct relevance to real-world problems in robotics, autonomous vehicles, and other domains where AI systems needed to make sequences of decisions in complex, dynamic environments. The success in Dota 2 demonstrated that reinforcement learning could scale to handle problems of genuine practical complexity.

OpenAI shared these results as part of its broader mission to ensure that artificial general intelligence benefits humanity. The research contributed to the understanding of how AI systems could be trained to handle tasks requiring strategic reasoning, teamwork, and adaptation to changing conditions, capabilities that would be essential for broadly beneficial AI systems.
