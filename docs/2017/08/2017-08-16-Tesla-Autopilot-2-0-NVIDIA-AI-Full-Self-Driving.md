# Tesla Autopilot 2.0 Pivots to NVIDIA AI for Full Self-Driving

📅 2017-08-16 | 📎 Tesla官方博客

<!-- 正文开始 -->
Tesla announced a major transition in October 2016, equipping all vehicles produced thereafter with Hardware 2.0, a sensor suite and compute platform designed to enable full self-driving capability. The system incorporated eight cameras providing 360-degree visibility around the vehicle, twelve ultrasonic sensors for close-range object detection, and a forward-facing radar capable of seeing through heavy rain and fog. This represented a decisive shift away from the camera-first approach of the original Mobileye-based Autopilot toward a more comprehensive perception architecture.

The new compute platform paired with this sensor suite was powered by NVIDIA's Drive PX 2 platform, delivering 12 teraflops of processing power specifically optimized for deep neural network inference. Tesla's neural networks processed inputs from all cameras simultaneously, running at 36 frames per second to enable real-time object detection, lane segmentation, and path planning. The dramatic increase in compute capacity allowed Tesla to train increasingly sophisticated models on the vast dataset collected from its growing fleet of vehicles.

Elon Musk revealed ambitious timelines for demonstrating full autonomy by the end of 2017, though these would prove overly optimistic as the complexity of the challenge became clearer. The transition to a camera-centric vision-only approach for the eventual release of Autopilot 9.0 in 2018 reflected lessons learned about the limitations of relying on sensor fusion with radar. Tesla's approach of collecting training data from hundreds of thousands of vehicles gave it an unprecedented advantage in training robust perception systems.

The Autopilot 2.0 announcement catalyzed competition in the autonomous vehicle space, with Waymo, Cruise, and traditional automakers accelerating their own programs. Tesla's approach of deploying partially automated features while collecting real-world data represented an innovative middle ground between the cautious regulatory-compliant strategies of competitors and Musk's frequently bullish public statements about timelines. The debate over the right balance between automation capability and safety assurance that Autopilot 2.0 sparked continues to shape the industry today.
