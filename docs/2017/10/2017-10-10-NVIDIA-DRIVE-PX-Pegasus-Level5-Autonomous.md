# NVIDIA Announces Pegasus Platform for Level 5 Autonomous Driving

📅 2017-10-10 | 📎 NVIDIA

<!-- 正文开始 -->
NVIDIA unveiled the NVIDIA DRIVE PX Pegasus platform in October 2017, describing it as the first AI supercomputer designed specifically for achieving Level 5 autonomous driving capability. The announcement represented a major step forward in the automotive AI industry, promising to deliver the computational performance necessary for vehicles that could operate without any human intervention in all environments and conditions.

The Pegasus platform combined the power of two NVIDIA Xavier GPUs with two new deep learning accelerators, delivering up to 320 trillion operations per second of AI performance. This represented a dramatic increase over the previous generation DRIVE PX 2 platform, which had been widely adopted by automotive manufacturers and technology companies working on autonomous vehicles. The enhanced computational capability was essential for processing the massive amounts of data flowing from cameras, lidar, radar, and other sensors in real-time while simultaneously running the complex neural networks required for perception, prediction, and decision-making.

One of the key innovations in Pegasus was its support for Level 5 autonomy, which required the system to handle all aspects of driving in any environment without human backup. This level of autonomy demanded not only raw computational power but also exceptional reliability and safety features. NVIDIA addressed these requirements with comprehensive automotive safety features including error correction code memory, isolated power rails, and comprehensive monitoring systems.

The platform was designed to support the transition to software-defined vehicles, where new capabilities could be added through over-the-air updates throughout the vehicle's lifetime. This approach aligned with the broader industry trend toward creating more flexible automotive platforms that could evolve and improve after initial deployment.

NVIDIA announced that major automotive suppliers including Bosch and ZF had committed to using the Pegasus platform in their autonomous vehicle systems. The company also revealed that several ride-hailing companies were planning to use the platform for fleets of autonomous vehicles that could operate without safety drivers under certain conditions. These partnerships demonstrated the growing momentum toward commercial deployment of fully autonomous vehicles.
