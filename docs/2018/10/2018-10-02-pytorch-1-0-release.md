# PyTorch 1.0 Release at NeurIPS

📅 2018-10-02

<!-- 正文开始 -->
Facebook announced the release of PyTorch 1.0 at the NeurIPS 2018 conference held in Montreal, marking a significant milestone in the evolution of one of the most popular deep learning frameworks in the research community. The release represented the culmination of months of integration work combining the best features of PyTorch and Caffe2, creating a unified framework that could seamlessly transition from research experimentation to production deployment. This release was particularly significant because it addressed one of the major pain points in deep learning development: the difficulty of moving models from exp[LoRA](../../glossary/terms/low-rank-adaptation.md)tory research into large-scale production systems.

PyTorch 1.0 introduced several important improvements including a unified framework that eliminated the need for code migration when moving from research to production. The integration of ONNX (Open Neural Network Exchange) as the default model export format allowed PyTorch models to be exported and used with other frameworks and deployment tools, greatly improving inte[RoPE](../../glossary/terms/rotary-position-embedding.md)rability across the deep learning ecosystem. Additional features included efficient graph-mode execution, comprehensive profiling capabilities, mobile deployment support, and integration with a wide range of vendor tools and hardware platforms.

The timing of PyTorch 1.0's release at NeurIPS, the largest AI conference of the year, underscored its importance to the research community. PyTorch had rapidly gained popularity since its initial release in 2017, becoming the framework of choice for academic researchers due to its intuitive dynamic computation graph and Pythonic design. The 1.0 release cemented PyTorch's position as a production-ready tool while maintaining the developer-friendly experience that had made it so popular in research. In the years following this release, PyTorch would continue to grow in importance, eventually becoming the most widely used deep learning framework in both research and industry, with adoption continuing to expand across academia and enterprise applications worldwide.
