# AlphaFold CASP13 Breakthrough

📅 2018-11-02

<!-- 正文开始 -->
DeepMind made its first major splash in protein structure prediction at the 13th Critical Assessment of Protein Structure Prediction (CASP13) competition held in November 2018, introducing AlphaFold to the scientific community. This marked the beginning of DeepMind's systematic effort to solve what many considered one of the grand challenges in biology: predicting protein 3D structure from amino acid sequences. AlphaFold's debut was remarkable, ranking first among 98 participants by correctly predicting the structures of 25 out of 43 proteins in the competition, establishing DeepMind as a serious contender in the field of computational biology.

The AlphaFold system relied on deep neural networks trained to predict two key properties of proteins: the distances between pairs of amino acid residues and the angles of chemical bonds connecting them. By analyzing patterns across thousands of known protein structures, the networks learned to extract evolutionary information from multiple sequence [Alignment](../../glossary/terms/alignment.md)s, using this to generate accurate structural predictions. The approach represented a significant departure from traditional methods that relied heavily on physics-based simulations, instead leveraging the pattern recognition capabilities of deep learning to capture the subtle relationships between sequence and structure.

The impact of AlphaFold's CASP13 performance extended far beyond the competition itself. The system's success demonstrated that deep learning could effectively tackle problems that had stumped researchers for decades, and it signaled DeepMind's commitment to applying AI to fundamental scientific challenges. Within two years, AlphaFold would achieve even more impressive results with its second version, and by 2021 the system would be recognized as one of the most significant AI achievements in science. The 2018 CASP13 appearance was the first chapter of a story that would fundamentally transform computational biology and drug discovery research worldwide.
