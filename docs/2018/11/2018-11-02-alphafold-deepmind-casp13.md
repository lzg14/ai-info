DeepMind announced in November 2018 their participation in the CASP13 (Critical Assessment of Protein Structure Prediction) competition and revealed that their AlphaFold system had achieved unprecedented accuracy in predicting protein 3D structures from amino acid sequences, solving one of biology's most fundamental challenges and demonstrating the potential for deep learning methods to accelerate scientific discovery in ways that could transform medicine, biotechnology, and our understanding of life itself at the molecular level.

AlphaFold approached the protein folding problem through a combination of novel deep learning architectures and physics-based optimization methods, predicting the distances between amino acid pairs and the angles between chemical bonds, then using these predictions to construct 3D structural models that satisfied the predicted geometric constraints while also minimizing physically implausible bond angles, lengths, and steric clashes that would render structures unstable in nature. This two-stage approach leveraged both the pattern recognition capabilities of neural networks and the principled optimization of molecular physics, combining data-driven learning with established scientific domain knowledge.

The protein folding problem had been recognized as one of the grand challenges in computational biology since the 1970s, with traditional methods relying either on comparative modeling based on known homologous structures or on fragment-based assembly methods that struggled to capture the complex long-range interactions governing how amino acid chains fold into their native conformations. AlphaFold's success demonstrated that deep learning could capture these complex relationships directly from sequence data, learning to recognize evolutionary patterns and structural motifs that encode the information needed to determine how proteins adopt their functional three-dimensional shapes, promising to revolutionize structural bioinformatics and drug discovery.
