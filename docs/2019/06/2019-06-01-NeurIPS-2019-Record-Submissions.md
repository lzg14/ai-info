# NeurIPS 2019 Sets Record with 15000 Submissions Amid Growing Compute Requirements

📅 2019-06-01 | 📎 NeurIPS官方统计

<!-- 正文开始 -->
## Content Summary

NeurIPS 2019, held in Vancouver from December 8-14, reached a historic milestone with over 15,000 paper submissions, nearly doubling the previous year's figure and establishing the conference as the premier venue for AI research worldwide. This exponential growth in submissions reflected both the rapid expansion of the AI research community and the increasing accessibility of deep learning tools that enabled more researchers to contribute to the field. However, the acceptance rate remained highly competitive at around 26.9 percent, with only 1,428 papers accepted, highlighting the intense competition for publication at the top venue.

The conference also revealed growing concerns about the environmental and economic costs of AI research. Training state-of-the-art models at NeurIPS required compute resources equivalent to hundreds of thousands of dollars, creating barriers for researchers from smaller institutions and developing countries. The carbon footprint of training a single large language model was estimated to be comparable to the lifetime emissions of five cars, prompting discussions about sustainable AI development. Researchers proposed solutions including more efficient architectures, better utilization of existing hardware, and increased sharing of pre-trained models to reduce redundant training.

Major highlights from NeurIPS 2019 included advances in reinforcement learning, generative models, and theoretical understanding of deep learning. The conference featured breakthrough work on language models that could perform tasks through few-shot learning, reducing the need for task-specific training data. Meta-learning research presented methods for training models that could adapt to new tasks with minimal fine-tuning, representing a step toward more general AI systems. The conference also saw increased attention to AI safety and fairness, with dedicated tracks exploring how to build AI systems that remained robust and unbiased across different populations and use cases.

The growth of NeurIPS also prompted discussions about the peer review process and paper quality. With submissions growing faster than the available pool of expert reviewers, concerns arose about the consistency and thoroughness of reviews. The conference experimented with various mechanisms to improve review quality, including dual-blind reviewing and post-acceptance discussion periods. These challenges would become increasingly important as AI research continued its rapid expansion, shaping how the scientific community evaluated and validated new discoveries.
