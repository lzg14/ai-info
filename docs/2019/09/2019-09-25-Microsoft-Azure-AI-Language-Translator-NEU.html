Microsoft Azure AI Achieves Human-Level Translation Quality on Chinese to English News Texts

Microsoft Research2019-09-25入库 2019-09-25

Microsoft announced that its Azure AI translation service had achieved human-level quality for Chinese to English translation of news texts, representing a significant milestone in natural language processing and machine translation capabilities. The accomplishment was the result of years of research combining the latest advances in neural network architectures, transfer learning techniques, and large-scale training data processing. This breakthrough demonstrated that AI could now match professional human translators for certain well-defined translation tasks.

The evaluation used professional translators to establish human baseline performance and then compared AI translations against this benchmark using both automatic metrics and human evaluation. The results showed that Microsoft's system produced translations that were statistically indistinguishable from human professional translations in terms of fluency and accuracy for news content. However, researchers emphasized that human-level performance on specific datasets did not mean AI had achieved general human-level translation capability across all domains and language pairs.

Technical advances driving the improvement included the development of new transformer-based models with specialized components for handling the significant word order differences between Chinese and English. Dual learning techniques allowed the system to train translation models in both directions simultaneously, improving overall quality. Integration of context beyond individual sentences enabled better handling of discourse-level coherence, important for longer documents.

Microsoft planned to incorporate the improved translation capabilities into its Azure cognitive services platform, making the technology available to enterprise customers building applications that required high-quality translation. The company also made models and techniques available through its research publications, enabling the broader research community to build upon the advances. Practical applications ranged from business communication and content localization to legal document translation and international customer service.

The achievement came amid intense competition among major technology companies to develop superior language understanding capabilities. Google, Amazon, and Baidu had all invested heavily in translation research, and the field had seen rapid progress in recent years. Microsoft noted that continued progress toward universal translation would require advances in handling low-resource languages, preserving nuance and cultural context, and maintaining performance across diverse subject domains.
