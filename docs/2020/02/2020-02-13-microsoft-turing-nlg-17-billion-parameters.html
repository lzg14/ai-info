Microsoft Unveils Turing NLG: A 17.8 Billion Parameter Language Model

Microsoft Research Blog2020-02-13入库 2020-02-13

In February 2020, Microsoft Research unveiled Turing Natural Language Generation (Turing NLG), a massive language model with 17.8 billion parameters, making it one of the largest language models at that time. This announcement intensified the competition among tech giants in developing ever-larger AI models for natural language understanding and generation tasks.

Turing NLG was developed using a deeply stacked architecture that leveraged the Megatron-LM framework for distributed training across thousands of NVIDIA GPUs. The model was trained on a diverse corpus of web content, books, and other text sources, enabling it to perform well across a wide range of language tasks including question answering, document summarization, and open-ended conversation.

Microsoft's research team demonstrated Turing NLG's capabilities in several practical applications. In question answering tasks, the model showed remarkable ability to provide accurate, detailed answers while citing relevant sources. For document summarization, Turing NLG could generate coherent abstracts that captured key information from lengthy texts, outperforming previous state-of-the-art approaches.

The development of Turing NLG highlighted Microsoft's commitment to advancing large-scale AI research and their investment in the infrastructure required to train such massive models. The company utilized its Azure cloud platform with specialized GPU clusters to enable the computational demands of training a model of this scale.

Turing NLG also demonstrated improved performance on the SuperGLUE benchmark, a collection of language understanding tasks designed to evaluate progress toward general-purpose language understanding systems. The model's strong performance across diverse tasks suggested that scale alone, combined with careful architecture design and training methodology, could unlock new capabilities in language AI.
