In May 2020, NVIDIA officially launched the A100 Tensor Core GPU, a revolutionary data center accelerator that became the foundational hardware for training the next generation of large language models and AI systems. The A100 introduced significant architectural improvements over its predecessor, the V100, delivering substantial performance gains across AI training, inference, and scientific computing workloads.

The A100 was built on NVIDIA's Ampere architecture and manufactured using TSMC's 7nm process with 54 billion transistors. It featured third-generation Tensor Cores that could accelerate matrix operations critical for deep learning training and inference. For AI training workloads, the A100 delivered up to 2.5x performance improvement compared to the V100, while inference performance saw up to 3x speedups depending on the workload type.

One of the A100's key innovations was its support for Multi-Instance GPU (MIG) technology, which allowed a single A100 GPU to be partitioned into up to seven independent instances, each with its own memory and compute resources. This capability was particularly valuable for inference deployments where different models could run simultaneously with guaranteed quality of service.

The A100 also introduced structural sparsity support, enabling models to use pruned weight matrices without losing accuracy while achieving up to 2x additional performance gains. This feature proved especially relevant for deploying large language models in production environments where efficiency was critical.

Within months of its launch, the A100 became the de facto standard GPU for training frontier AI models. Major cloud providers including AWS, Google Cloud, and Azure rapidly adopted A100 instances, and it became the preferred hardware for training models like GPT-3, Claude, and other large language models that emerged in subsequent years. The A100's impact extended beyond AI to scientific computing, climate modeling, drug discovery, and autonomous systems development.
