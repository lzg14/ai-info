MLP-Mixer: An All-MLP Architecture for Vision Tasks Challenges CNNs

arXiv2020-08-04入库 2020-08-04

Researchers from Google Research published the MLP-Mixer architecture, a novel approach to computer vision that relied exclusively on multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs), without using convolutions or attention mechanisms. The paper challenged the assumption that complex operations like convolution or self-attention were essential for achieving high performance on visual tasks.

MLP-Mixer's architecture was based on mixing features at token locations and channel dimensions through simple matrix multiplications. Despite its simplicity, the model achieved competitive accuracy on image classification benchmarks when trained on large datasets, reaching over 87% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet.

The research was part of a broader trend in exploring alternative architectures to transformers and CNNs. It demonstrated that the success of large-scale training could overshadow the importance of inductive biases built into architectures, raising interesting questions about the nature of learning in neural networks.

MLP-Mixer inspired further research into simpler architectures and contributed to the growing body of work showing that transformer-like performance could be achieved through various mechanisms. While CNNs and Vision Transformers remained dominant in practice, MLP-Mixer expanded the design space for neural network architectures and sparked important discussions about efficiency and expressiveness in model design.
