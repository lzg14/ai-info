DeepMind AlphaFold2 Solves the Protein Folding Problem: A 50-Year Grand Challenge

Nature2020-11-30入库 2020-11-30

DeepMind's AlphaFold2 system achieved a breakthrough that scientists had described as impossible, virtually solving the protein folding problem—a challenge that had occupied biologists for half a century. The achievement was announced during the 14th Critical Assessment of Protein Structure Prediction (CASP14) competition, where AlphaFold2 demonstrated unprecedented accuracy in predicting the 3D structure of proteins from their amino acid sequences.

Proteins are the machinery of life, and their function is determined by their 3D shape. Understanding protein structures is crucial for drug discovery, understanding diseases, and developing new therapies. Traditional methods for determining protein structures, such as X-ray crystallography and cryo-electron microscopy, are expensive and time-consuming, often taking years to determine the structure of a single protein. AlphaFold2 could predict these structures in hours or minutes, potentially revolutionizing the field of structural biology.

The system used a novel approach based on attention mechanisms, drawing on advances in natural language processing to model the relationships between amino acids and their positions within protein chains. The deep learning architecture was specifically designed to understand the physics of protein folding, learning the underlying rules that determine how proteins adopt their functional configurations. What distinguished AlphaFold2 was not just its accuracy but its reliability, consistently producing high-quality predictions even for proteins with no known similarities to previously characterized structures.

Demis Hassabis, DeepMind's founder and CEO, described the achievement as potentially the most significant scientific advance of the decade. The implications for medicine were profound—researchers could now theoretically predict the structure of any human protein and use this information to design drugs that precisely target disease-causing misfoldings. The system was also expected to accelerate research into diseases involving protein misfolding, including Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, and various cancers.

DeepMind made the bold decision to release the system openly, providing free access to AlphaFold2 through Google's cloud platform. This decision reflected the company's mission to use AI for scientific discovery and benefit humanity. Within days of release, the database contained predicted structures for nearly every protein coded in the human genome, a resource that researchers compared to having a complete map of the human body at the molecular level.
