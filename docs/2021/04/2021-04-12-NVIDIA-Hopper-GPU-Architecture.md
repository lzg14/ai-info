# NVIDIA Announces Hopper GPU Architecture for AI

📅 2021-04-12 | 📎 NVIDIA官方博客

<!-- 正文开始 -->
In March 2022, NVIDIA unveiled its Hopper GPU architecture, named after computing pioneer Grace Hopper, delivering substantial improvements in AI training and inference performance compared to its predecessor Ampere generation. While the official launch occurred in 2022, the announcement and preview of Hopper technology in 2021 signaled NVIDIA's continued dominance in AI hardware and set new benchmarks for the industry. The flagship H100 GPU, built on the Hopper architecture, introduced new features specifically designed for large language model training and deployment, including the Transformer Engine that provided significant speedups for models like GPT-3.

The H100 GPU featured 80 billion transistors and offered up to 3x faster training performance for transformer models compared to the A100, driven by architectural improvements including enhanced CUDA cores, increased memory bandwidth through HBM3, and dedicated hardware for FP8 precision computing. The Transformer Engine was particularly significant as it allowed models to automatically switch between FP16 and FP8 precision during training, maintaining accuracy while dramatically accelerating computations for the increasingly large transformer-based models that were becoming standard in the industry.

Beyond individual GPU performance, the Hopper architecture introduced the NVLink Switch System and NVIDIA Base Command Platform enhancements that enabled unprecedented scale-out capabilities for AI supercomputing clusters. The architecture was designed from the ground up for the emerging requirements of large language models, autonomous driving systems, and enterprise AI applications, representing NVIDIA's vision for the computational infrastructure needed to support the next generation of AI advances.

The impact of Hopper extended beyond raw performance metrics, as the architecture's focus on efficiency reflected growing concerns about the environmental and economic costs of training massive AI models. NVIDIA emphasized both absolute performance and performance-per-watt improvements, responding to criticism about the carbon footprint of large-scale AI computing. The Hopper launch also intensified competition in the AI chip market, with AMD's Instinct series and custom silicon from Google, Amazon, and other hyperscalers vying for position in an increasingly competitive landscape.
