NVIDIA Launches Hopper GPU Architecture for AI Computing

NVIDIA官方博客2021-04-12入库 2021-04-12

NVIDIA unveiled its Hopper GPU architecture in April 2021, representing a new generation of AI-focused computing hardware designed to accelerate machine learning workloads dramatically. Named after computer science pioneer Grace Hopper, the architecture introduced numerous innovations specifically optimized for the computational demands of modern AI systems, including transformer models and large language models.

The flagship H100 GPU, built on the Hopper architecture, offered unprecedented performance for AI training and inference tasks. The H100 featured dedicated Transformer Engine hardware that could accelerate the attention mechanisms central to models like GPT-3 and similar transformers. The architecture also introduced new precision formats including FP8, allowing for faster computation while maintaining model accuracy through automated precision management.

The Hopper architecture's significance extended beyond raw performance improvements. NVIDIA emphasized the architecture's ability to handle the growing computational demands of frontier AI models, which had been doubling approximately every two years. The H100 could be clustered in massive configurations, with NVIDIA's DGX SuperPOD systems providing thousands of GPUs working together to train the largest AI models. This computational power became essential for research institutions and companies pursuing cutting-edge AI development.

Major cloud providers including AWS, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Azure quickly adopted the Hopper architecture, offering H100-based instances to customers. The architecture also supported NVIDIA's enterprise AI software stack, including frameworks for training, fine-tuning, and deploying AI models. The launch of Hopper reinforced NVIDIA's dominant position in AI computing hardware and set new standards for the specialized accelerators that power modern artificial intelligence research and applications.
