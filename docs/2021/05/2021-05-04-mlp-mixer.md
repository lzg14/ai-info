Google Research published a paper introducing MLP Mixer in May 2021, an alternative architecture to transformers and convolutional neural networks that achieved competitive performance using solely multi-layer perceptrons. The paper, titled "MLP-Mixer: An all-MLP Architecture for Vision," demonstrated that the success of transformers in vision tasks was not solely due to attention mechanisms but could be achieved through simpler linear transformations applied across spatial locations and feature channels.

MLP Mixer replaced attention layers with two types of MLP operations: channel mixing and token mixing. Channel mixing operated independently on each spatial location, while token mixing operated across spatial locations for each channel. This simple architecture achieved remarkable performance on image classification benchmarks, reaching nearly 90% accuracy on ImageNet when properly trained with modern techniques like heavy data augmentation and regularization.

The paper contributed to discussions about the fundamental components driving transformer success in vision. Some researchers argued that attention was not as crucial as previously thought, while others pointed out that MLP Mixer's performance relied heavily on the same training techniques that made transformers successful. The architecture sparked interest as a potentially more efficient alternative for certain applications, though later research showed it struggled with higher-resolution images compared to hierarchical transformers.

MLP Mixer was part of a broader trend in exploring simpler alternatives to complex neural network architectures. Google's investigation into whether attention mechanisms were truly necessary for vision tasks yielded important insights about the role of cross-token interactions and channel-wise transformations. The work influenced subsequent research into more efficient vision models and contributed to the growing understanding of what made transformers so effective across different domains.
