# DeepMind AlphaFold2 Solves Protein Folding Problem

On July 15, 2021, DeepMind made a historic announcement that sent shockwaves through the scientific community. The company released an open-source version of AlphaFold 2, their breakthrough deep learning neural network for protein structure prediction, described in a paper published in Nature. The network had achieved remarkable results in the CASP14 protein structure prediction competition the previous year, and now DeepMind was making the technology freely available to researchers worldwide. This achievement was described by the competition's co-founder as effectively solving a problem that had challenged scientists for over 50 years.

AlphaFold 2 addresses the fundamental challenge of predicting how proteins fold into their three-dimensional structures. Proteins are linear sequences of amino acids that fold into complex 3D shapes, and understanding these structures is crucial for comprehending protein function and enabling drug discovery. Traditional experimental methods like X-ray crystallography, nuclear magnetic resonance, and cryo-electron microscopy are both time-consuming and expensive. AlphaFold 2 uses an advanced neural network architecture, combining multiple sequence [Alignment](../../../glossary/terms/alignment.md) information with distance constraints between amino acid residues, to predict protein 3D structures with accuracy approaching experimental methods but at speeds far exceeding traditional laboratory techniques.

Following the initial release, DeepMind partnered with EMBL-EBI (Eu[RoPE](../../../glossary/terms/rotary-position-embedding.md)an Molecular Biology Laboratory) to launch a protein structure database where researchers can search for specific proteins and instantly receive 3D structural images. In July 2021 alone, AlphaFold predicted the structures of 350,000 proteins, covering nearly all human proteins and those from 20 other organisms including E. coli, yeast, and fruit flies. By the end of 2021, DeepMind expanded this to 200 million predicted protein structures—reaching half of all known proteins in the human body. This massive database release has been transformative for structural biology, enabling researchers worldwide to access protein structure data that would have taken decades and enormous resources to determine experimentally. The AlphaFold 2 system was later awarded the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, cementing its status as one of the most significant scientific AI breakthroughs in history.
