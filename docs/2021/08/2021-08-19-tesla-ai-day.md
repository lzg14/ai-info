# Tesla AI Day Showcases Dojo Supercomputer and Neural Network Advances

📅 2021-08-19 | 📎 Tesla Blog

<!-- 正文开始 -->
Tesla held its inaugural AI Day in August 2021, presenting an ambitious vision for its artificial intelligence capabilities centered around autonomous driving, robotics, and custom hardware. CEO Elon Musk and Tesla's AI team revealed detailed technical information about their neural network architectures, training infrastructure, and the Dojo supercomputer system designed specifically for AI training at scale. The event was part talent recruitment drive and part demonstration of Tesla's AI ambitions.

The star of the event was Dojo, Tesla's custom-built supercomputer designed for training autonomous driving neural networks. Tesla's director of hardware engineering, Ganesh Venkataramanan, described the D1 chip built on a 7-nanometer process that could deliver 22.6 teraflops of BF16/CFP8 performance. Dojo combined thousands of these chips into a seamless training cluster with unprecedented bandwidth between nodes, allowing Tesla to process massive amounts of video data from its fleet of vehicles.

Tesla also unveiled its planning and control systems for Full Self-Driving technology, demonstrating how the company used neural networks to understand and navigate complex driving scenarios. The team explained how they were using simulation, shadow mode driving, and data collection from the real world to continuously improve their models. A detailed walkthrough of the HydraNet architecture showed how Tesla shared features across multiple tasks like object detection, lane prediction, and traffic light recognition.

The event included a glimpse of Tesla's upcoming robot, officially named Optimus, which Musk described as a general-purpose humanoid robot that could eventually address labor shortages. The robot prototype was shown moving cautiously on stage, representing early but promising progress in Tesla's robotics ambitions. AI Day demonstrated Tesla's commitment to vertical integration in AI, building custom silicon and software to maintain competitive advantages in autonomous systems.
