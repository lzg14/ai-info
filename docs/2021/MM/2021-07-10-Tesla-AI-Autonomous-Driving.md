Tesla held its inaugural AI Day in August 2021, presenting a comprehensive overview of its artificial intelligence capabilities for autonomous driving and robotics, headlined by the announcement of the Dojo supercomputer designed specifically for training neural networks at unprecedented scale. CEO Elon Musk and Tesla's Autopilot team detailed the company's approach to achieving full self-driving capability, which relied primarily on camera-based vision systems rather than LIDAR or HD maps, arguing that human drivers navigated the world using only visual input and therefore machines should be able to do the same with sufficiently advanced AI. The event showcased Tesla's custom Hardware 4 chips, improvements in the neural network architecture powering its FSD system, and simulation tools used to generate synthetic training data.

Central to Tesla's presentation was theDojo system, a distributed training supercomputer comprised of thousands of custom-designed chips optimized for the matrix operations central to neural network training. Tesla claimed Dojo would provide the computational power needed to train its vision-based autonomous driving networks on billions of video frames collected from its fleet of vehicles, enabling continuous improvement in driving capability through over-the-air software updates. The system represented a significant vertical integration strategy, with Tesla designing its own silicon rather than relying on commercial GPU suppliers to optimize specifically for its training workloads.

Tesla also revealed early prototypes of its humanoid robot, the Tesla Bot, demonstrating the company's belief that advances in autonomous driving AI could transfer to general-purpose robotics. The robot, intended to handle dangerous, repetitive tasks, would share sensing and navigation technology with Tesla's vehicles, leveraging the investments made in FSD development for broader applications. While the announcement was met with skepticism from some robotics experts who questioned whether driving AI directly translated to humanoid robot control, it signaled Tesla's ambitions beyond vehicles and highlighted the increasing convergence of automotive and robotics AI research.

The AI Day event served multiple purposes for Tesla, including recruitment of AI talent in a competitive market and demonstration to investors of serious technical progress toward autonomous vehicles. The company's approach of collecting real-world data from its existing fleet gave it advantages in training scale but also criticisms regarding the deployment of experimental driver assistance features on public roads. Tesla's progress remained controversial, with some claiming the company overstated capabilities while others defended its vision-first approach as more scalable than LIDAR-dependent competitors.
