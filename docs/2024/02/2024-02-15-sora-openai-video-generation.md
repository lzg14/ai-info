# OpenAI Sora: Revolutionary Text-to-Video AI Model

On February 15, 2024, OpenAI officially released Sora, its first text-to-video large language model, once again shocking the global AI industry. Sora can generate high-quality video content up to 60 seconds long based on text descriptions. The generated videos feature highly realistic details and coherence, making it difficult for many people to distinguish whether the content was created by AI.

Sora's name comes from the Japanese word "空" (sora), meaning sky. The OpenAI team stated that they chose this word because it evokes unlimited creative potential. Unlike traditional video generation tools, OpenAI positions Sora as a "world simulator," demonstrating artificial intelligence's ability to understand and generate the visual world.

The model inherits OpenAI's technological accumulation in natural language processing and image generation, capable of creating extremely realistic video scenes from users' descriptive text, from human movements to lighting effects, all vivid and lifelike. Sora's release is viewed as a major breakthrough in the AI video generation field, expected to have profound impacts on multiple industries such as film production, advertising, gaming, and education. Technical experts indicated that this breakthrough will redefine the boundaries of content creation.

Sora's underlying technology is based on the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture, combining the principles of [Diffusion Model](../../../glossary/terms/diffusion-model.md)s with [Transformer](../../../glossary/terms/transformer.md) architectures. Unlike previous video generation models that could only produce short clips, Sora can generate complex scene sequences with multiple camera angles, character interactions, and emotional expressions. More importantly, Sora demonstrates partial capabilities of a world simulator—it can understand the physical rules of the real world to some extent, such as gravity, object occlusion relationships, and light reflection variations.

However, Sora has not yet been publicly opened for use. Currently, it is only available to red team researchers and some creators for safety evaluation and feedback collection. OpenAI acknowledged that Sora still has some weaknesses, such as difficulties in accurately simulating complex physical processes and occasional confusion in spatial details. Nevertheless, industry observers generally believe that Sora marks a pivotal moment in AI video generation technology, and as the model continues to iterate and improve, its impact on related industries will be even more profound.
